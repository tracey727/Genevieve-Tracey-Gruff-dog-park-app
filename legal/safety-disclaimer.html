Safety Disclaimer

Version: 3 August 2026 · trip-routing update

No app, score, colour, route or search result can guarantee safety or service availability. Dog behaviour, health, people, weather, surfaces, fencing, traffic, roads, ferries, park conditions and provider details can change immediately.

Risk and attention colours

One is green and lower calculated risk or planning attention; ten is red and highest. Green never means “safe”. Journey’s green route status means that places and road geometry were calculated, not that roads, weather or stops are safe. Roadside route markers remain amber until a lawful, signed, dog-safe stopping place is checked. Incorrect, incomplete or outdated inputs produce unreliable guidance.

Owner and trip responsibility

Keep the dog actively supervised and under effective control. Observe body language, use distance, follow signs and council rules, and leave when conditions deteriorate. Check-in is voluntary community information, not admission, reservation, monitoring or professional supervision.

On a trip, stop earlier for heat, stress, toileting, illness, traffic or driver fatigue. A route coordinate is not a safe rest-area recommendation. Verify current external directions, road closures, legal stopping access, water, shade, surfaces, ferries, pet transport, accommodation and veterinary support. Never leave a dog unattended in a vehicle.

Health and heat

Heat and hazard results are general prompts, not veterinary diagnosis. Stop activity and seek veterinary help for collapse, breathing distress, confusion, severe panting, vomiting, injury or other urgent symptoms.

Emergency and animal services

The persistent Emergency Help control requires a three-second hold before opening the services hub. A user may enter a place or explicitly request current device location to build Google Maps searches for nearby vets, council animal services, pounds, shelters, rescues, wildlife help and other providers. Search results, distances, hours and telephone details must be checked directly. GENEVIEVE does not dispatch, book, contact or confirm a service.

For life-threatening emergencies in Australia call Triple Zero (000). The in-app 000 control requires its own three-second hold and a separate final slide before asking the device to open the dialler. It does not call on the user’s behalf, transmit location or confirm that help is coming.

Accessibility communications

Large-text communication cards are plain-language aids. They are not Auslan interpretation, emergency dispatch or a substitute for a qualified interpreter.

Professional safety review required before launch: veterinarian/animal behaviour review of heat, compatibility and trip-break calculations, accessibility review, emergency-control real-device testing and Australian legal review.
